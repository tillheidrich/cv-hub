Answer in the language of the job ad. If the ad is in English and does not ask for German, send an English CV. If it is in German, send a German CV, even if the job itself is partly in English. That rule covers most cases. The rest of this article explains the exceptions and what the numbers say about English-speaking jobs in Germany.

## How common are English-only jobs?

Less common than many applicants hope. Indeed's Hiring Lab analysed job ads on Indeed in Germany from September 2023 to August 2024: only 2.7 percent stated that no German was required. In software development the share was about 3.7 percent, higher than in most fields but still small. Among the European countries in the study, only Ireland had a lower share. These are platform data from one job site and measure a single feature of the ads, so treat them as an indication, not a census.

At the same time, Make it in Germany treats documents in English as normal: certificates may be translated into German or English. Nobody official says an English CV is wrong. The question is what the specific employer expects.

## Signals in the job ad

**An English CV is usually fine when:**

- the ad is written in English,
- it names English as the working language ("Our working language is English"),
- the company is international, a Berlin or Munich start-up, or a tech company with teams in several countries,
- German is listed as "a plus" or not mentioned.

**A German CV is the safer choice when:**

- the ad is in German, even with English technical terms,
- it asks for German at a level ("Deutschkenntnisse B2", "verhandlungssicheres Deutsch"),
- the role involves customers, public authorities or German documentation,
- the employer is a traditional company, a Mittelstand firm, a public institution or a hospital.

If you are unsure, the cover letter can settle it: write the CV in the language of the ad and mention your German level in the first paragraph.

## Do I need both versions?

If you apply to both kinds of employers, yes. Keep the content identical and translate only the language: same dates, same jobs, same bullets. Don't translate job titles that a company used officially in English, such as "Senior Software Engineer". Do translate section headings, dates and descriptions. A German CV with English headings looks unfinished.

Get the German version proofread by a native speaker. The Federal Employment Agency recommends this explicitly in its guide for international applicants, and a machine translation that is technically correct can still sound odd.

## When language is the real problem

Many international developers report dozens of applications without a reply. Discussions among them repeatedly come back to one point: German. A good CV in perfect English does not help if the team wants German in meetings. If you hear nothing back, check how many of the ads you answered asked for German, and consider a course. On a German CV, an honest "German: A2, B1 course until 06/2027" is better than nothing, because the employer can see you are working on it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it OK to apply in English in Germany?

Yes, when the job ad is in English or names English as the working language. For ads in German, a German CV and cover letter are expected, even in tech.

### Should my CV be in German if the job ad is in English?

No. Answer in the language of the ad. Mention your German level in the CV and, if it is good, in the cover letter.

### Can I use a machine translation for my German CV?

As a first draft, yes. Have it checked by a native speaker before you send it, as the Federal Employment Agency recommends. Mistakes in a German CV stand out more than in an English one.
