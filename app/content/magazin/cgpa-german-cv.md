Write your original grade and add the German equivalent next to it, calculated with the modified Bavarian formula: "CGPA 8.2/10 (German equivalent 2.0)". German grades run the other way round from most foreign systems: 1.0 is the best, 4.0 the lowest pass. Without a conversion, a recruiter in Germany has to guess what your number means, and many won't take the time. This article explains the formula, why there is no fixed table for Indian grades, and how to write the result on a CV.

## The German grading scale

German universities grade from 1.0 to 4.0 in steps such as 1.3, 1.7 and 2.0. 1.0 means "very good", 4.0 is the lowest passing grade, and anything worse is a fail. A CV with "2.1" next to the degree is read in seconds. A CV with "CGPA 8.2" is not.

## The modified Bavarian formula

German universities and the admissions service uni-assist convert foreign grades with a formula agreed by the Standing Conference of the Ministers of Education (KMK):

```text
German grade = 1 + 3 × (Nmax − Nd) / (Nmax − Nmin)
```

- **Nmax** is the best grade possible in your system, for example 10 on a ten-point CGPA scale or 100 percent.
- **Nmin** is the lowest grade with which you pass the whole degree.
- **Nd** is your grade.

The result lies between 1.0 and 4.0. According to the KMK resolution it is given to one decimal place without rounding, so 2.08 becomes 2.0 and 1.97 becomes 1.9.

## Why there is no single table for India

Nmax is easy, Nmin isn't. Indian universities do not use one common minimum pass grade, and the value can differ between institutions, programmes and years. uni-assist asks applicants from India to document the grading system of their own university, including the best grade and the minimum for passing the degree ("Pass Class" or "Third Division"). The pass marks of single courses don't count.

That is why the same CGPA leads to different German grades:

| Your grade | Nmax | Nmin (assumed) | German equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| CGPA 8.2 | 10 | 5.0 | 2.0 (2.08) |
| CGPA 8.2 | 10 | 4.0 | 1.9 (1.90) |
| 72 percent | 100 | 40 | 2.4 (2.40) |

The minimum grades in this table are examples, not facts about any university. German universities take the reference values from the ZAB's assessment recommendations in anabin or from the grading document of your university, which uni-assist asks applicants from India to submit. For your CV, find the value in your transcript, your university's examination rules or the grading scheme on your degree certificate. Websites that promise "CGPA 8 = German 1.8" for everyone skip exactly this step.

## How to calculate your grade

1. Find Nmax and Nmin for your degree. Look at the back of your transcript or in your university's regulations.
2. Put the numbers into the formula. Example with CGPA 8.2, Nmax 10, Nmin 5: 1 + 3 × (10 − 8.2) / (10 − 5) = 1 + 3 × 1.8 / 5 = 2.08, which gives 2.0 because the second decimal is cut off.
3. Write down the values you used. A recruiter or a university may want to check them.

## How to write it on your CV

Put both grades on one line, with the method in brackets:

```text
2013 – 2017   B.Tech Computer Science, Pune
              CGPA 8.2/10 (German equivalent 2.0,
              modified Bavarian formula, minimum pass grade 5.0)
```

Shorter is fine if space is tight: "CGPA 8.2/10 (2.0 on the German scale)". Keep the original; the conversion is an aid, not a replacement. If you have an official conversion, for example from a German university that admitted you to a master's programme, use that value and say so.

## Grades and degree recognition are two different things

A converted grade says how good you were. It says nothing about whether your degree counts as equivalent to a German one. For that, German employers and visa offices look at the anabin database of the KMK (university listed as "H+" and degree as equivalent) or at a Statement of Comparability from the ZAB, the Central Office for Foreign Education. For IT jobs no formal recognition is needed, but for the EU Blue Card the comparability of the degree has to be shown, unless you apply as an IT specialist without a degree. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good German grade?

Most German examination regulations use these bands: 1.0 to 1.5 very good, 1.6 to 2.5 good, 2.6 to 3.5 satisfactory, 3.6 to 4.0 sufficient. Many job ads in Germany do not ask for a specific grade, but some graduate programmes do.

### Can I just use an online CGPA to German grade converter?

Only if it lets you enter the minimum pass grade of your own university. A converter with a fixed minimum gives a number that may be wrong for your degree.

### Should I convert my Class X and XII marks too?

No. Once you have a degree, school results rarely matter on a CV for Germany. Convert the grade of your highest degree.

### Does the ZAB convert my grades?

The ZAB Statement of Comparability describes and classifies your degree; it does not convert your grade. Universities calculate a German grade during admission, usually with the modified Bavarian formula.
