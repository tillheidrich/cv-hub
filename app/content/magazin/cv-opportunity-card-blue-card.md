For a visa to Germany, your CV has to match your documents exactly. Every point you claim for the Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) and every year of experience you need for an EU Blue Card has to be backed by a certificate or a reference, and the dates on your CV should be the same as on those papers. Add one line on your visa status so employers know where you stand. This article lists the 2026 rules that affect your CV and how to reflect them.

It is not legal advice. The figures come from Make it in Germany, the Federal Employment Agency and the Federal Gazette; the German mission in your country decides on your application.

## The EU Blue Card in 2026

The Blue Card is for people with a university degree comparable to a German one and a job offer with a minimum salary. For 2026 the thresholds are:

- **50,700 euros** gross per year in general,
- **45,934.20 euros** for shortage occupations, for graduates whose degree is at most three years old, and for IT specialists without a degree.

IT specialists without a degree qualify with at least three years of relevant professional experience at university level within the last seven years. Below 50,700 euros, the Federal Employment Agency has to approve the employment.

**What this means for your CV:**

- Your degree with the comparability you have checked: "B.Tech Computer Science (anabin H+, equivalent)" or "ZAB Statement of Comparability, 2026".
- For IT without a degree: every job with month and year, so the three years in the last seven can be counted at a glance. The job descriptions should show that the work was at the level of a graduate, for example architecture decisions, system design or technical lead.
- No salary on the CV. The salary is in the job offer, not in your application.

## The Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte)

The Opportunity Card lets you come to Germany for up to one year to look for a job. If your degree or vocational qualification is fully recognised in Germany, you qualify directly. Otherwise you need at least six points. The basic requirements are a foreign degree or at least two years of vocational training that is state-recognised in the country where you obtained it (shown via anabin or the ZAB), German at A1 or English at B2, and proof that you can support yourself (1,091 euros net per month in 2026).

Points according to Make it in Germany (as of 12.11.2026):

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Partial recognition of your qualification | 4 |
| Qualification in a shortage occupation | 1 |
| 2 years of professional experience in the last 5 years | 2 |
| 5 years of professional experience in the last 7 years | 3 |
| German A2 / B1 / B2 or higher | 1 / 2 / 3 |
| English C1 or native | 1 |
| Age up to 35 / 35 to 40 | 2 / 1 |
| Legal stay of at least 6 months in Germany in the last 5 years (not as a tourist) | 1 |
| Partner who also qualifies | 1 |

With the card you may work up to 20 hours a week while you search and do trial work of up to two weeks per employer.

**What this means for your CV:** Each line on your CV that earns a point must match a document. If you claim five years of experience, the dates on your CV should add up to five years and agree with your references. If you claim German B1, write the certificate and the year: "German B1 (Goethe-Zertifikat, 2026)". A CV that says B2 while your certificate says B1 raises questions, at the employer and at the embassy.

## The status line

German employers are often unsure how hiring someone from abroad works. One line in your personal details answers the first question:

- "EU Blue Card holder, valid until 08/2028"
- "Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte), in Germany since 01/2026"
- "Eligible for an EU Blue Card (degree comparable, anabin H+)"
- "Skilled worker visa required; recognition procedure in progress"

Only write what is true. "Eligible" should mean you have checked the degree and the salary range for the job.

## Documents that go with the CV

Make it in Germany lists the cover letter, the CV and references as the usual application documents, with certificates translated into German or English. For the visa itself, the German mission has its own list. The Blue Card checklist of the German missions in India, for example, lists "CV and job description (one page only)", plus the anabin printout or the ZAB statement. Check the current list of your mission before you apply; that checklist dates from 2023.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I mention my visa status on my CV?

Yes, in one line in the personal details. "EU Blue Card holder" or "Opportunity Card, in Germany since 01/2026" saves the employer a question and shows that you know the route.

### What is the Blue Card salary threshold in 2026?

50,700 euros gross per year in general and 45,934.20 euros for shortage occupations, recent graduates and IT specialists without a degree, according to the announcement in the Federal Gazette of 18.12.2025.

### Can I get a Blue Card in IT without a degree?

Yes, with at least three years of relevant professional experience at university level within the last seven years and a job offer above the lower threshold. Your CV should show those years with exact dates.

### How many points do I need for the Opportunity Card?

Six, unless your qualification is fully recognised in Germany, in which case you qualify without points. You also need a qualification recognised in the country where you obtained it, German at A1 or English at B2, and proof of funds.
